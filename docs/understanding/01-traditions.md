# 1. The traditions and how they differ

Source keys refer to [bibliography.md](bibliography.md). **[F]** = read this session, **[M]** = from
background knowledge and not re-verified. Claims marked [M] should be checked before being relied on.

## The shared skeleton

All lineages work from the same canonical formula [SU, M]: secluded from sense desire and
unwholesome states, the meditator enters four successive jhanas, then four formless attainments.

| | Canonical description | What is present | Simile (DN 2 / MN 39) |
|---|---|---|---|
| J1 | Rapture and happiness "born of seclusion", with applied and sustained attention | vitakka, vicāra, pīti, sukha (+ one-pointedness in the commentaries) | Bath powder kneaded with water until the ball is soaked through |
| J2 | Rapture and happiness "born of concentration"; inner stillness | pīti, sukha, unification | A lake fed from within by a spring |
| J3 | Rapture fades; "equanimous and mindful, dwelling happily" | sukha, equanimity | Lotuses immersed in cool water |
| J4 | Neither pleasure nor pain; "purity of mindfulness by equanimity" | equanimity, unification | A person wrapped head to foot in white cloth |
| J5–J8 | Infinite space → infinite consciousness → nothingness → neither-perception-nor-non-perception | progressively less | none |

**The five jhana factors** and the hindrance each is said to oppose [VM, M]:
applied attention (vitakka) ↔ sloth and torpor; sustained attention (vicāra) ↔ doubt;
rapture (pīti) ↔ ill will; happiness (sukha) ↔ restlessness and worry; one-pointedness (ekaggatā)
↔ sense desire. **Equanimity (upekkhā)** takes over from J3.

**Access concentration (upacāra samādhi)** is a commentarial term [VM, M] for the state just
before absorption: hindrances quiet, attention continuous. Brasington's working definition is
being "fully there" with the object while thoughts are wispy and in the background [B1, F].

**A causal chain is already in the suttas.** The Upanisa Sutta [SU, M] gives: gladness → rapture
→ tranquillity → happiness → concentration. AN 11.2 [SU, M] says each step arises from the one
before with "no need for an act of will". The traditional texts describe a self-propagating
process that is set up and then allowed, which is the core of the model in
[04-mechanism.md](04-mechanism.md).

## The big split: how deep counts as jhana

Sparby & Sacchet reviewed eight modern manuals [S13, F] and concluded that they describe three
different depths under one word:

| Type [S13] | Starts when | Representative manuals |
|---|---|---|
| **Light** | Attention is fairly stable and any jhana factor (such as joy) is present | TWIM (Johnson); Yates' broadest definition |
| **Intermediate** ("sutta jhana") | "A phenomenological shift where attention locks onto the meditation object" and the factors are present | Brasington; Ingram; Yates' pleasure jhanas |
| **Deep** ("Visuddhimagga jhana") | The mind fully absorbs into a nimitta; from J2, external perception, body and thought are gone | Pa-Auk / Snyder & Rasmussen; Shaila Catherine; Ajahn Brahm; Dennison |

They list five standing disagreements [S13, F]: whether hindrances are reduced or wholly gone;
whether there are thoughts in jhana; whether one can deliberately think or investigate inside
it; whether a sense of self remains; and whether a nimitta is required. The same paper offers a
general definition that all eight manuals share [S13, F]: stable concentration and absorption;
positive valence; restful and still, with little or no thought; **clear awareness, as opposed to
trance or sleep**; and a progression in which factors refine.

This project targets the **intermediate** type: it is the one fast-teaching retreats reach in a
week, and it has a clear onset criterion.

## Lineage by lineage

| Lineage | Object | Entry move | On effort | Time claimed | Check |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ayya Khema → Leigh Brasington** | Breath to access; then a pleasant sensation (hands, heart, face) | At access, drop the breath and attend to the *pleasantness* of a pleasant sensation; "do nothing else" | Do not try to make it grow, do not "help it"; expectations are the worst thing to bring | Typically on a 9–10 day retreat; no promise | B1 F |
| **Rob Burbea** | Energy body, whole-body breath, mettā | Find any pīti, then alternate a narrow "probing" attention with an open, receiving one, and spread it through the body | Effort is a dimmer; be willing to overshoot and undershoot to learn it. Ask "can I enjoy it more?" and not "can I make it stronger?" | Long retreat; mastery by "marinating" at one's edge | RB1, RB2 F |
| **Jhourney** | Embodied loving-kindness and deliberate relaxation; "a feeling meditation" | Evoke an open-hearted feeling, rest in it, re-evoke when it fades | Over-efforting is "one of the most common mistakes" | "Well over half" of participants within a week (their figure) | J1, J2 F |
| **TWIM (Vimalaramsi)** | Mettā | Radiate mettā; on every distraction, release, relax, smile, return ("6Rs") | Rejects forceful concentration; a light resting on the feeling | Days on retreat | S13, S14 F; details M |
| **Pa-Auk / Visuddhimagga** | Breath at the nostrils until a light nimitta appears and stabilises | Shift to the stable nimitta; absorb fully; then the "five masteries" | Sustained, continuous, all-day effort; nimitta must not be chased | Months to years; many never do. One account: 12 days at 14–20 h/day to hold J1 for 45 min, after years of practice | FP8 F; PA M |
| **Ajahn Brahm** | "Beautiful breath" → nimitta | Let go of the "doer"; the nimitta draws the mind in | Any willing or wanting blocks it; first jhana "wobbles" as the mind grasps at the bliss and lets go | Long retreats | S13 F; AB M |
| **Thanissaro Bhikkhu** | Whole-body breath energy | Make the breath comfortable, spread comfort through the body | Evaluating and adjusting is part of J1; wary of deep, frozen absorption | Gradual | TB M |
| **Culadasa (TMI)** | Breath through ten stages | Whole-body, then pleasure, then luminous jhanas as depth increases | Effortlessness arrives at Stage 7–8 | Months to years of daily practice | TMI M; S13 F |
| **Daniel Ingram (MCTB)** | Any | "Soft" to "hard" jhana as a continuum of the same states | Pragmatic | Varies widely | MCTB M |

## Moving between the first four (Brasington) [B2, F]

- **Into J1:** the pleasant sensation builds, unevenly, and then takes off into an unmistakable
  state. Stay one-pointed on the pleasure.
- **J1 → J2:** shift attention from the physical pleasure to the emotional joy, so the physical
  energy drops into the background. J2 is "much more soothing".
- **J2 → J3:** let go of the physical pleasure and turn the joy down into quiet contentment.
- **J3 → J4:** let go a little more, into neutral calm with no positive or negative feeling.

Each step is a *release* of the coarsest thing present. This is the same structure as the
canonical "with the stilling of…" formula.

## What every fast-working lineage agrees on

1. **A pleasant bodily feeling is the working object** once the mind has settled. Where it comes
   from varies (breath comfort, hands, smile, mettā, memory) and should be found per person:
   Brasington reports that smiling works for only about a quarter of his students [B1, F].
2. **The instruction at the critical moment is to do nothing.** Brasington: observe the
   pleasantness and nothing else [B1]. Burbea: open and enjoy [RB1]. Ajahn Brahm: remove the doer
   [AB, M]. Jhourney: the main error is over-efforting [J2].
3. **Relaxation is an active ingredient, not a warm-up** (TWIM's relax step, Jhourney's
   "intentional relaxation", Burbea's receptive mode).
4. **Wanting it is the main obstacle.** Expectation [B1], grasping at the bliss [AB via S13],
   craving and comparison [FP6, FP7].
5. **Jhana is bright.** All manuals contrast it with trance, dullness or sleep [S13, F].

## Where they really disagree (and what this project does about it)

| Question | Lighter view | Deeper view | Position taken here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Is "jhana in a week" jhana? | Yes: the factors are present and the shift is real [B1, FP6] | No: it is "mindfulness of the jhana factors" at best [FP6]; real jhana has no sense perception | Use Sparby & Sacchet's three types and state which is meant (intermediate) |
| Thought in J1? | Some wispy thought is compatible (vitakka-vicāra as thinking) [B3 M; S13 F] | None; vitakka-vicāra is attention, not thought | Require thought to be sparse and non-capturing, not absent |
| Body and sound present? | Yes, the body is where the pleasure is | Gone from J2 at the latest | Present |
| Nimitta needed? | No | Yes | No |
| Is it risky to teach fast? | Risks are low and comparable to other retreats [FP9 concession; J3] | Needs close, experienced supervision; ~100 h teachers are inadequate [FP9] | Screen, warn, stop early; see `docs/validation.md` |
| What is it for? | Wellbeing, emotional processing [J6]; "human, not Buddhist" [J4] | A platform for insight; without that aim, of little value [FP9] | Out of scope; the app teaches the skill and says so |

## Gaps in this map

- Pa-Auk, Ajahn Brahm, Thanissaro, Culadasa and Ingram are described from memory and from how
  S13 summarises them, not from their texts.
- Burbea's talk on effort, view and attitude (the second half of RB1) was not captured.
- Brasington's guidance on over-strong pīti and on physical shaking was not found on the pages
  read; the line taken in the app (shift from the buzz to the calmer happiness, which is the J2
  move) follows B2 and is otherwise [M].
