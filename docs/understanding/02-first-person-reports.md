# 2. First-person reports: what recurs

**Evidence base, stated plainly.** 20 individual accounts are coded in
[`kb/reports.json`](../../kb/reports.json): 19 read in full this session, 1 seen only as a search
snippet. They come from one retreat write-up, one Substack essay, one essay on the deep path,
blog comment round-ups, a Hacker News thread and a LessWrong thread. Aggregate claims are in
[`kb/aggregates.json`](../../kb/aggregates.json). This is a small, self-selected, English-language,
tech-adjacent sample; a larger forum sweep was planned and did not complete (`docs/log.md` #6).
Counts below are out of these 20 and show which way the evidence points, not how common things
are in general.

Report ids are in `code font`.

## 2.1 What people were doing just before entering

Only 5 accounts describe the lead-up, and they agree:

- Deeply relaxed, then holding a joyful image and letting the joy "loop" (`asparouhova-2024`).
- Keeping the pleasure in *secondary* attention and treating jhana "like falling asleep":
  arrange conditions and let it happen (`lw-quickstart-author`).
- Attending narrowly to a pleasant sensation "until a feedback loop builds" (`chapin-2022`).
- Not trying at all: the deepest event came during an unrelated breathing exercise
  (`hn-three-years`).
- Brasington's instruction matches: at access, attend to the pleasantness "and do nothing else"
  [B1].

Nobody reports entering while pushing.

## 2.2 Blockers, by frequency

| Blocker | Accounts | What resolved it |
|---|---|---|
| **Grasping / trying to get it** | 5: `lw-quickstart-author`, `acx-tetris`, `brasington-own`, `asparouhova-2024` ("pushed harder than ideal"), `hn-twim-and-vm` (craving and comparison) | Relaxing effort; treating it like sleep; stopping for the day when frustrated |
| **Not enough dose or conditions** | 4: `acx-romeo` (only on retreat), `wystan` (no retreat conditions), `lw-niplav` (day 21), `acx-bugmaster` (dropped out) | Retreat; many hours a day |
| **Self-doubt, inner critic, not trusting it** | 3: `chapin-2022`, `asparouhova-2024`, `acx-todd` | Self-acceptance; for Chapin, access "came from getting over my self-hatred", not from concentration training |
| **Fear at a threshold** | 1: `wystan` (anxiety when breath sensation vanished). Also a student in Burbea's Q&A, frightened by a bodily movement [RB2] | Reassurance that it is normal; familiarity |
| **Difficult emotion surfacing** | 2: `hn-dread`, plus guilt about feeling joy in Burbea's Q&A [RB2] | Stop; kindness; do something else for a while |
| **Too much energy** | 1: `acx-tetris` (first jhana "too much pleasure") | Normalises with practice; move toward the calmer states |

Teachers name the same top item. Jhourney: over-efforting is "one of the most common mistakes"
[J2]. Brasington: do not try to make it grow or "help it" [B1]. Ajahn Brahm describes first
jhana as wobbling because the mind grasps at the bliss and lets go [S13].

**New to the theory from this reading:** self-criticism is a blocker in its own right. It is not
the same as checking for results. It was missing from the first version of the coach and has been
added (`docs/log.md` #8).

## 2.3 False starts and misidentifications

- **Pīti without jhana.** `lw-quickstart-author` and `brasington-own` could raise some pīti but
  not enter: pleasure present, loop not closing.
- **Altered but not pleasant.** `lw-goenka`: dissolving body boundaries, "liquid", not bright or
  pleasant. Insight-style body scanning produces this.
- **Mild contentment labelled jhana by a teacher.** `acx-todd`: told he had been in jhana;
  it felt only "kinda nice".
- **Label disputes from the deep side.** HN commenters call the retreat-style J1 "not even access
  concentration" and suggest "mindfulness of the jhana factors" as the honest name [FP6]. Others
  suspect a blank state (bhavanga) is being mistaken for jhana [FP6].
- **Calibrated uncertainty exists.** `hn-unsure` describes joy settling into stable contentment
  with thoughts disappearing, and still declines the label without a teacher.

How communities adjudicate "is this jhana?": by tradition-specific depth criteria (sense
perception gone or not), by whether the factors are present, and by whether there was a distinct
shift. There is no shared standard, which is what Sparby & Sacchet's three-type scheme tries to
fix [S13].

## 2.4 What "it clicked" felt like

- A sudden take-off after uneven growth: grows, pauses, grows, then "takes off" [B1]; builds
  until it "explodes into an unmistakable state" [B2]. One HN commenter passes on the comparison
  to a sneeze [FP6].
- Tingling in hands and chest as the herald (`asparouhova-2024`); "sharp, tingly, electric"
  (`chapin-2022`); "basically MDMA" (`acx-tim`); "full body orgasm" at depth (`acx-romeo`).
- The shift in attitude is always the same direction: from doing to allowing. "Relax, have fun,
  and don't overthink it" (`asparouhova-2024`). Asparouhova adds a useful refinement: the target
  is closer to *flow* than to relaxation, which matches the requirement that jhana be bright.

## 2.5 How the states differ, in lay terms

| | Recurring descriptions |
|---|---|
| J1 | Euphoric, bright, electric, tingling, can be agitating or "too much" |
| J2 | Gratitude, beaming joy; more soothing; the energy is in the background |
| J3 | Contentment, soft, quiet |
| J4 | Stillness, neutral, restful more than pleasurable; one writer says "dissociative" |
| J5–J8 | Space, then successively less; "barely consciousness" |

Two accounts say people come to prefer the calm states to the euphoric one (`chapin-2022`; the
Jhourney alumni survey [J5]).

## 2.6 How long it took

| Account | Context | Time to first jhana |
|---|---|---|
| `asparouhova-2024` | Retreat with coaching | ~2¼ hours of practice, day 1 |
| `zerfas-engineer` (second-hand) | Retreat | A few days; a few dozen lifetime hours |
| `lw-quickstart-author` | Solo | <10 sitting hours, spread over ~9 months |
| Jay Michaelson's estimate [FP9] | Retreat | 40–50 hours; 3–4 days experienced, 5–7 days new, at 8–10 h/day |
| `lw-niplav` | Month retreat | Day 21; >1,000 hours in total; unreliable after |
| `hn-three-years` | Solo, from a book | ~3 years |
| `wystan` (deep jhana) | Monastery, then Pa-Auk retreat | Seconds-long touches in months; stable after 12 days at 14–20 h/day |
| `hn-jhourney-nonresponder`, `acx-bugmaster`, `hn-dread` | Mixed | Never |

The spread covers about three orders of magnitude in hours. Retreat-with-coaching accounts
cluster at days. Solo accounts cluster at months to years, with one fast exception.

Group figures, all from Jhourney or its participants, none with a stated definition of success:
"well over half" [J2]; about two-thirds (project brief); about 75% on one retreat by a
participant's count, with the caveat that some arrived already able to enter [FP6].

## 2.7 Who gets there fast, who does not

What the accounts themselves point to:

- **Prior meditation hours do not predict it.** The fastest account had ~30 lifetime hours; a
  year-long daily practitioner on the same kind of retreat did not get there while near-novices
  did (`hn-jhourney-nonresponder`); >1,000 hours bought unreliable access (`lw-niplav`). Jhourney
  says the best predictor is "mindset and ownership" [J2], and its survey found experience did
  not affect benefit [J5].
- **Relationship to oneself matters.** Self-hatred as the lock, self-acceptance as the key
  (`chapin-2022`). Guilt about joy [RB2]. An HN commenter argues access depends on arriving with
  a mind not full of negativity [FP6].
- **Conditions matter.** Retreat, hours per day, and a teacher who reads your reports recur as
  the difference (`acx-romeo`, `wystan`, [FP9]).
- **A habit of forceful concentration may hurt.** This is the natural reading of experienced
  meditators failing where novices succeed, and Jhourney's site is aimed at people for whom
  meditation "still feels like a grind" [J1]. It is an inference, not something measured.

Not found in what was read: any data on trait absorption, hypnotisability, aphantasia, ADHD or
interoception as predictors. These stay hypotheses (see [07-open-questions.md](07-open-questions.md)).

## 2.8 Harms and afterglow

- **Afterglow** fades over days to weeks [J5]; energy can persist into the next morning and
  disturb sleep for a night or two (`lw-quickstart-author`).
- **Jhourney's safety report** [J3]: about 3% of ~1,200 participants had transient difficulty
  (sleep disruption, emotional intensity, unfamiliar sensations, disorientation, dissociation),
  typically resolving in days. One participant with no psychiatric history developed acute mania
  needing psychiatric care, with recovery over about four months. Jhourney says it cannot
  reliably predict who is at risk.
- **Quiet can surface distress** in some people even at beginner doses (`hn-dread`). Two HN
  commenters describe months of imbalance after intensive insight practice by app [FP6]; that is
  not jhana practice, but it is the neighbouring risk.
- Critics argue the supervision is too thin for the intensity [FP9].

## 2.9 The sceptical case

1. **Self-report is weak evidence**, and there are social rewards for claiming unusual
   attainments [FP3].
2. **Exaggeration.** The "10–100× better than sex" framing [FP2] is disputed by other
   practitioners in the same thread, who report "nice", "flat and artificial" after a while, or no
   change in other desires [FP3, FP5].
3. **Definition drift.** Fast-taught jhana is a lighter state than the classical one [FP6, FP9].
4. **Priming.** What people experience may follow what the instructions lead them to expect
   (`lw-goenka`).

Scott Alexander's reply to (1) is that jhana breaks no physics and should be believed about as
much as reports of migraine or MDMA [FP3]. The replies to (3) are that both strong and weak
versions are real and useful [FP6], and that the terminology can simply be made explicit [S13].
Points (2) and (4) stand. They are the reason this project's scoring uses a conjunction of
criteria and foil items, and why the study design in `docs/validation.md` uses a blind rater.
