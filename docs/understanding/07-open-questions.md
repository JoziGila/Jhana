# 7. Open questions and disagreements

## Between traditions

| Question | Why it matters for a product | Current handling |
|---|---|---|
| **Is fast-taught jhana "jhana"?** Deep-jhana practitioners say it is at best mindfulness of the jhana factors [FP6]; others call that a language game [FP6]; Sparby & Sacchet propose three named types [S13] | Determines what success means and whether the name is honest | Say "intermediate (sutta-style) first jhana" and define it operationally |
| **Thought in first jhana.** Vitakka-vicāra as thinking [B3] or as attention [S13] | Whether residual thinking disqualifies a sit | Sparse, non-capturing thought allowed |
| **Does it need supervision?** Traditional teachers say close, experienced supervision is essential [FP9]; Jhourney reports about 3% transient difficulty and one serious event in ~1,200 [J3] | Whether an unsupervised app is responsible at all | Screening text, stop rules, gentle defaults. Unresolved: see `docs/validation.md` |
| **What is it for?** Insight [FP9], wellbeing and emotional change [J6], or nothing much beyond itself [FP5] | Framing and retention | The app teaches the skill and takes no position |
| **Is the pleasure overstated?** "10–100×" [FP2] versus "kinda nice", "flat after a while" [FP3, FP5] | Expectations are themselves a breaker | Under-promise: describe early jhana as often gentle |

## About the mechanism

1. **Threshold or continuum?** The model says bistable. Light-jhana accounts describe gradual
   onset [S13]. Decisive data: time-stamped in-sit ratings from many first entries.
2. **Is grasping a cause or a symptom?** An experiment that manipulates only the onset
   instruction ("when it grows, do nothing") would separate these.
3. **Is permission a separate condition?** Self-criticism and guilt appear as blockers that
   concentration does not fix [FP5, RB2]. If so, kindness or forgiveness practice should help
   the guarded subgroup specifically and not others.
4. **Reward circuit or prediction?** One-subject fMRI supports reward involvement [S1];
   hierarchical-deconstruction accounts fit the later jhanas [S4, S18]. Possibly both, at
   different stages.
5. **What does the afterglow depend on?** It is reported to last days to weeks [J5] and to
   disturb sleep [FP7]. Dose-response is unknown.
6. **Are light and deep jhana the same process?** Dennison's slow-wave picture [S2] and the
   high-complexity picture [S4, S6] look different.

## About measurement

7. **Are the EEG "complexity" signatures neural or muscular?** In the open dataset the
   Lempel-Ziv and slope effects scale with smile-muscle activity across people
   (`eeg/results/REPORT.md`). The published single-subject findings could share this confound.
   A decisive check: repeat with EMG-regressed or source-localised data, or in fourth jhana
   where smiling is rare.
8. **Is there any marker of the transition itself in beginners?** No recording of a first-ever
   entry exists in the literature read.
9. **Does breathing rate track entry, or only depth of relaxation?**

## About who succeeds

10. **Which traits predict speed?** Candidates with no data yet: trait absorption [S24],
    hypnotisability, interoceptive accuracy, music-evoked chills, self-criticism, striving.
11. **What is the true success rate, by a fixed definition, with no coach?** Every figure
    available comes from one provider with unstated criteria [J2].
12. **How much of a retreat's effect is dose?** Would the same instructions at one hour a day
    get there in proportionally more days, or not at all?

## About this project's own bets

13. **Does between-sit diagnosis work without a human?** The central bet. Untested.
14. **Do in-sit taps help or interrupt?** They give the guide its cue to go quiet, but any task
    during absorption is a form of doing.
15. **Do the foil items discriminate?** They were chosen by judgement.
16. **Does measuring progress, even on a ladder, feed the wanting it is meant to avoid?**
