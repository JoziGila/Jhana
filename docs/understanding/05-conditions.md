# 5. Necessary and sufficient conditions, and who gets there fast

Scope: the **intermediate** first jhana of [01-traditions.md](01-traditions.md). Everything here
follows from the loop model in [04-mechanism.md](04-mechanism.md). The conditions are a
hypothesis about what the evidence implies, stated strongly so that it can be tested.

## 5.1 Six conditions

| # | Condition | Loop term | Why it is necessary | If missing, you get |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | **A seed.** Some reliably available pleasant bodily feeling | Starting value of pleasure | The loop amplifies; it does not create | Calm with nothing happening |
| C2 | **Continuity.** Attention can stay on a subtle object for tens of seconds at a time | Attention | Only continuous attention amplifies | A feeling that flickers and never builds |
| C3 | **Ease.** Body and face soft; effort light and falling | Tension | Tension multiplies the gain down | A tight, dry, steady focus; headaches |
| C4 | **Non-interference at the onset.** No grabbing, pushing, measuring or bracing when it grows | Grasping | Grasping scales with the pleasure, so it can cap the loop below threshold indefinitely | Repeated take-offs that collapse |
| C5 | **Brightness.** Awake, interested | Energy | Slack attention dissolves; jhana is defined against sleep [S13] | Pleasant drowsiness |
| C6 | **Time above threshold.** Enough undisturbed minutes for growth to run | Duration | Growth near threshold is slow | "Almost" sits that end too soon |

**Claim of joint sufficiency.** If C1–C6 hold together for long enough, first jhana follows
without any further act. This is what "no need for an act of will" [SU, M] and "do nothing else"
[B1] assert. The claim is falsified by people who meet all six, by careful interview, and still
do not enter.

**A seventh candidate: permission.** Feeling safe and feeling allowed to feel good. Chapin's
access "came from getting over my self-hatred" [FP5]; a retreatant's guilt about joy blocked
pīti [RB2]; Jhourney teaches forgiveness practice for emotional "blocks" [J4]. In the model this
acts through C3 and C4 (guarding is tension; self-criticism is a form of checking). It may deserve
to be a condition of its own. See [07-open-questions.md](07-open-questions.md).

## 5.2 What is *not* necessary

- **Years of practice.** People with 30 lifetime hours [FP1] and "a few dozen" [J6] have
  entered within days; the open dataset includes an expert with 300 lifetime hours [S14].
- **A nimitta, or loss of body and sound.** Those belong to deep jhana [S13].
- **A particular object.** Breath, hands, mettā, smile and memory all serve [B1, RB1, J2].
- **Buddhist belief or ethics.** Disputed: some practitioners hold that a settled conscience
  matters a great deal [FP6], which is compatible with C3/C4 acting through guilt and agitation.

## 5.3 Why some beginners are fast and others never get there

The model says speed depends on which condition is binding for a person, and on whether anyone
notices which one it is.

| Profile | Binding condition | Typical story | What unlocks it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Warm and unguarded.** Easily feels affection; gets chills from music; low self-monitoring | None | Enters within hours once shown where to look [FP1] | Being shown |
| **The striver.** Long history of effortful concentration; treats meditation as work | C3, C4 | Excellent attention, no take-off; "a grind" [J1]; sees novices succeed around them [FP6] | Halving effort; no-goal sits |
| **The grabber.** Excitable; wants it badly | C4 | Pīti starts and collapses, again and again [FP3, FP7] | Being told in advance to do nothing; repetition until the onset is familiar |
| **The checker.** Analytical; reads a lot about jhana | C4 (attention on the idea, not the feeling) | "Is this it?" throughout; discounting mild states | Process goals; less reading; savouring |
| **The guarded.** Self-critical, or tense about pleasure | C1, C3, permission | Flat, numb, "nothing there"; or sadness surfaces [FP5, FP6] | Kindness practice first; gentlest seed; not pushing |
| **The sleepy.** Relaxes straight into drowsiness | C5 | Pleasant fog, lost time | Posture, eyes, time of day, interest |
| **The scattered.** Little practice at staying with anything | C2 | Never settles | More time on an easy anchor; counting |
| **Under-dosed.** Any profile, 10–15 minutes a day | C6 | Slow or nothing | Longer sits; a home retreat day |

This table is the reason prior experience "barely predicts success": experience raises C2, which
is rarely the binding condition for adults who can read a book, and it often *lowers* C3 by
training a habit of force. The reason retreats work is that they raise C6 enormously, lower the
hindrance load, and supply a coach who finds the binding condition.

## 5.4 What a retreat gives that an app has to replace

| Retreat ingredient | Function in the model | App substitute | How good a substitute |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6–8 hours a day [J2] | C6; hindrances settle | Longer sits; a home-retreat-day mode | Poor. The biggest gap |
| Daily 1:1 review of your journal [J1] | Finds the binding condition | Post-sit debrief → diagnosis → changed plan | Unknown: this is the bet |
| Seclusion; nothing to do | Low hindrance load; less goal pressure | Pre-sit check; no-goal framing | Partial |
| Seeing others succeed | Belief; reduces doubt | None in this prototype | Missing |
| A person present if things go wrong | Safety | Warnings, stop rules, signposting | Weak |

## 5.5 Predictions that could be tested cheaply

1. The rung where a person stalls predicts which fix helps: reducing effort helps strivers and
   harms the scattered.
2. Telling people in advance to do nothing at the onset reduces collapses on the first take-off.
3. A short questionnaire about warmth, music chills, striving and drowsiness predicts sits to
   first take-off better than hours of prior meditation.
4. Trying several seeds in the first sits and keeping the best beats a fixed mettā seed for
   people who score low on "easily feels warmth".

These are the hypotheses behind the study in `docs/validation.md`.
